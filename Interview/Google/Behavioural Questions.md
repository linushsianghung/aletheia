## Behavioural Questions
A behavioral question looks at how candidates have handled a specific challenge in the past to assess if they’ll be a good match for the role.

Usually start with phrases such as:
- Tell me about a time when ...
- Give me an example of ...
- Describe a decision you made ...

Using **CARL** approach:
- **Context**
- **Action**
- **Result**
- **Learning**

**Using **STAR** approach:**
- **Situation**: Describe the situation you were in or the task you needed to accomplish
- **Task**: Explain the goal you were working toward
- **Activity**: Detail the specific steps you took and the role(s) you played
- **Result**: Describe your accomplishments and the overall outcome

> Specific for Customer Solutions Engineer (CSE):
> 
> The focus of this part of the interview will be on your stakeholder management and consulting skills. Your interviewer will want to see your consulting skills and understand how you work with customers and partners through both hypothetical and behavioral questions: how you uncover and assess stakeholder needs through conversations in order to make recommendations. They’ll want to see that you effectively engage, collaborate with, and influence stakeholders by forging collaborative relationships. Show how you manage expectations and agreed objectives while remaining engaged and authentic, regardless of personality, technical ability, or background.

## Hypothetical/Situational Questions
Hypothetical questions evaluate how candidates would handle a challenge they may not have encountered yet. Questions often begin with “Imagine that ..."

### Questions:
- **What would you do if requirements are unclear?**
  > It's necessary to truly understand what their problem is. The main principle of here is that provide what they need rather than what they want. So ...

----------
- **What would you do when someone is being left out**
  This is an `Inclusive` and `Problem Solving (Show how you think rather than give an Example)` question which should be response by using **CFS (Clarify, Framework, Solution)** method.
  - **Clarify**:
    - Clarify the context
      - Is it about a new or existing member?
      - What's my relationship whit the member?
      - How long have I been working with the member?
      - Do I personally have a good relationship with the member?
    - Is this member always being left out or just recently behaviour shift?
    - Is there any impacts for this member and the entire team and what are the impacts so far, like timeline, deadline, group relation, etc.?
    - Is this during some Edge Scenario, like Covid-19?
  - **Framework**: More specifically, using more `Collaborative Framework`!
    - Using collaborative terms and concepts: `Communication`, `Meeting Cadence`, `Listening, Questioning`, `Empathy`, `Trust Input`, `Action Plan`, `Collaboration`, etc.
  - **Solution**: The most important point is **Put the Interviewer in the room**.

> [Name of interviewer], let's call this person Bob. First, I would have a few questions about Bod. I'd want to know if Bob is new to the team or project and has Bob historically always been left out or is this a recent trend? I'd want to understand a little more about my relationship with Bob like is he internal or external and how long I have been working with Bob? And lastly I want to understand the overall impact. Does it seem to be strongly impacting only Bob or the entire team? Sorry for a lot of questions for you but it's necessary to me to provide a more accurate solution.
> 
> OK! Let's consider few important items and I definitely want to focus on `communication` first. Obviously it's going to be critical to have some serious `listening`, some `questioning`. I would be coming with empathy and trust to create an `action plan`. I think all of this are important points that we should focus on. How about we start with communication part does that work for you?
> 
> Great! My first step is going to be to communicate with the team members and focus on 2 specific items, namely their perspective, feedback and any missing information that I don't know. Then secondarily, I would say I'd love to take the lead on it so I am getting people's permission to move forward to take this action plan on with Bob. Next just casually ping to Bob to have an easy talk and ideally meet Bob face to face. And start with the warming fuzzies like how this week going? do you have any fun plan recently? And then I would shift to what's going on at work and trying to figure out the resourcing pieces. At some points after these questions, I might provide some personal experience, resources and suggestions to Bob. And hopefully Bob can be open to it and ideally we can come up with an Action Plan to move things forward. I think it's a great place to pause and we could definitely dive into some additional questions that I might as Bob or talk about the action plan or something else that you would be interesting in.

- **What are your weaknesses?**
  - Don't make that about the personality but about the skill set which can be worked on.
  - Issue Of personality usually can't be fixed but skill set can be improved.
  - Use CAR (Context-Action-Result) Formula

  > One of my weaknesses I really concern about is I have a poor memory. it doesn't mean I often forget something important or  join a meeting that sort of thing. What I mean is that comparing with some of my amazing colleagues, who can remember issues around 1 year or even 2 years ago clearly and describe that in detail. So when we had a meeting or discussed some issues, they can response that really quickly.
  > 
  > In order to eliminate this gap, what I always do is trying to document every case I handle before in detail. For instance, when I address a issue of one of our services, I write down the context of that issue so I can have a high level view first, like who found this and what was the wrong behaviour of that service. And then record how I investigated the issue and what steps I did to find out the root cause. Third, what I did to solve the problem and how I verified the solution can really work.
  >  
  > As a result, although I still cannot response for a topic immediately during the discussion, but I can rely on my documents and designed key words to search what I want quickly and then provide my experience for the problem. Because of this weakness, I kind of develop one of my strength, namely documenting, accidentally and I get benefit a lot from that.

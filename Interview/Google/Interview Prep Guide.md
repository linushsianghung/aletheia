# Customer and Partner Solutions Engineer interview prep guide
## Interview Process:
Recruiter Call → **3 Rounds of Interviews** → Offer Review → Offer Delivery
- 60 min `Tech Role Related Knowledge (RRK) 1` - Database/Storage-specific
- 60 min `Tech Role Related Knowledge (RRK) 2` - Coding & System Design
- 60 min `Non-Tech Role Related Knowledge` + `Googleyness & Leadership (G&L)`

## Interview focus areas:
### Tech Role Related Knowledge (RRK) 1 - Domain Specific:
**_Focus on this for the first round!_**

This interview will test your **Storage & Database-specific acumen**, ability to troubleshoot issues as well as related experience through technical fundamental questions.
The interviewer will be assessing the following areas related to the Networking domain:
- Structured, logical thinking when approaching problems.
- Deep foundational knowledge in database administration (MySQL, PostgreSQL, NoSQL) and object storage (GCS, S3).
- Understanding of high-availability architecture patterns for databases.
- Practical, hands-on experience that goes beyond theory.

### Tech Role Related Knowledge (RRK) 2 - Coding & System Design:
This interview will be conducted using your preferred coding language, **Python/Java/Go/C++**.
This interview will assess your ability to:
- Write structured code, with good understanding of appropriate data structure and algorithms.
- Debug and test to ensure code health.
- Design reliable large-scale systems, understand pitfalls of the design, make recommendations to customers' workload architecture for optimization and/or reliability improvements.

### Non-Tech Role Related Knowledge (RRK) + Googleyness & Leadership (G&L):
**Non-Tech RRK**: The focus of this part of the interview will be on your stakeholder management and consulting skills. Your interviewer will want to see your consulting skills and understand how you work with customers and partners through both hypothetical and behavioral questions: how you uncover and assess stakeholder needs through conversations in order to make recommendations. They’ll want to see that you effectively engage, collaborate with, and influence stakeholders by forging collaborative relationships. Show how you manage expectations and agreed objectives while remaining engaged and authentic, regardless of personality, technical ability, or background.

**G&L**: To help you prepare for this, think of examples of times you have used your communication and decision-making skills to mobilise others - this could be people on your team, people around you cross-functionally, or your boss above you.

The interviewer will be assessing the following areas:
- Googleyness
    - Thrives in Ambiguity
    - Values Feedback
    - Effectively Challenges the Status Quo
    - Puts the User First
    - Does the Right Thing
    - Cares About the Team

- Leadership
    - Manages Projects
    - Gets Things Done
    - Works as a Team
    - Strives for Self Development

**Googleyness** can sometimes feel like a vague concept, but we like to test certain behaviours and biases to action that you have to ensure you are the right culture for being successful with us.  Some of those areas that we look at include your ability to work in a fast-paced, changing environment and how you handle ambiguity. We might ask you about ethical decisions or situations you have faced, your ability to value different perspectives different from your own, and your ability to challenge the status quo when necessary. This might be around a decision that you didn't agree with, or when you received resistance from someone on your team — how did you manage that? We are also likely to ask you questions that revolve around putting the user first, or a situation about the way you care about your team and focus on doing the right thing.

**Leadership** for us is defined as stepping up in your job to get things done and to take ownership of a project or a process. In summary, make sure you prepare yourself by considering the following information:
- Examples of success, accomplishments, and achievements in your career
- Examples of difficult problems you solved, or a difficult challenge you had to face
- Examples of working in a team and working by yourself, where you were able to learn something new
